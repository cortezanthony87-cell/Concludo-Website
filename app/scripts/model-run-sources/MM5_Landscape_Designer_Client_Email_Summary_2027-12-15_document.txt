Subject: Follow-up from yesterday's site visit at Elm Street
Date: Wednesday 15 December 2027

Dear Rhys,

Thank you for having me out to the property yesterday and for the coffee. It was good to walk the whole block with you and to see how the back slope drains after the storm. Here is where I think we landed, in the order we talked about it.

1. Retaining wall. We agreed that the timber sleepers at the back are past their life and the wall should be replaced with stone. I have put the wall at about $9,500 installed. I'd treat that as a rough figure until the stonemason has seen it, because it depends on how deep the footings need to go.

2. Paving. You preferred the grey bluestone-look pavers over the sandstone for the courtyard. I'll price that at around $3,200 for the area we measured. I have not yet had a firm quote from the supplier, and they are the only supplier I use for that range, so if they run short in January the lead time could blow out.

3. Planting. We decided to keep the existing eucalyptus and remove the two dead wattles by the side fence. I'm proposing native grasses along the slope, which should cut down on the mowing you mentioned. I wonder, honestly, whether you really want to keep mowing that strip at all.

4. Total. In my notes at the site I wrote the total as $14,200. After checking the figures against the measurements at my desk this morning, that was wrong. The correct total for items one to three is $14,800, including my design fee. Please use the $14,800 figure and disregard the $14,200.

5. Council permit. A retaining wall of that height will need a permit. If the council approves it before the middle of January, I can start on Monday 17 January. If it is not through by then, I would push the start back to February. I can't promise the January date until the permit is in your hands.

6. Next steps. I'll send the formal quote by Friday 24 December. I'd like you to confirm the pavers and the permit application by the end of the first week of January. Would you like me to organise the permit for you, or would you rather lodge it yourself? Let me know which you prefer.

7. Photos and notes. I took about forty photos of the slope and the existing wall during the visit. Would it be all right with you if I shared them, along with my site notes, with the stonemason so that he can quote more accurately? I would only send them to him and would not use them for anything else. I'll hold off until I hear from you.

I know the end of the year is busy for you and Marguerite. We can talk it through on the phone any time on the weekend if that is easier.

Warm regards,
Zainab Okonkwo
Okonkwo Garden Design, Warrandyte
