Group coaching session, Wednesday 6 October 2027. My notes (Rosalind Pereira), written the same evening.

Present: Anh Tran (Tran Landscape Design), Giuseppe Ferraro (Ferraro Picture Framing), Naomi Whitcombe (Whitcombe Physiotherapy), and me. Ninety minutes in my studio. It was raining hard, so everyone arrived late and wet, and we spent the first ten minutes drying off and talking about the footy.

Consent. I asked at the start whether I could write these notes and keep them in the shared folder for the group. Anh and Naomi said yes. Giuseppe said he was happy for me to keep notes for myself but did not want them in the shared folder. I agreed and will keep his section separate. I did not ask whether I could name Naomi's clinic in my case study for the coaching network. I will ask her next time before I use it. Nobody mentioned recording and I did not record.

Anh. She opened with the news that she has decided to take on a second crew. I asked what changed her mind. She said the waiting list is now about nine weeks and she is losing quotes to a bigger firm. Naomi said she thought nine weeks was too long, and Giuseppe agreed. So all three of us think the delay is costing her work. Anh then said she wanted to buy a second truck as well, which I think is premature. Her budget for the truck was sixty thousand dollars. She corrected that later in the session. It is actually forty-five thousand, because the figure she gave me first included the trailer she already owns. I have used forty-five thousand in my notes. She will get three quotes for a used truck by Friday 15 October. I told her not to sign anything with the dealer before she has seen all three. She agreed.

The risk with Anh is that one person, her foreman Marcus, knows how every client site is laid out and all the council permits. If Marcus left, she would lose months. We talked about writing site notes. She said she will start with the five largest sites and finish them by the end of November. If Marcus leaves, I am not sure what she would do. I told her it was worth asking him about his plans, but she said she was nervous about doing that. We left it.

Naomi, on hypotheticals. She asked what would happen if she moved the clinic to bigger premises across the road. The rent would be around eight thousand a month, maybe more. I said we could not decide that on a guess. She said she would only do it if two more practitioners joined. That is a big if.

Giuseppe. His section is separate, as agreed. The main news is that framing orders from the art gallery are about half of his income, and the gallery owner has said he may reduce the number of shows next year. That is a real dependence on one client. Giuseppe said, "I have never lost a customer I wanted to keep." That is a claim I would not repeat without evidence. He is thinking of starting a small online shop for ready-made frames. I suggested he try ten frames first. He said it was a good idea and he will run a test with ten frames in November. Naomi told him she thought the idea was brilliant. I think so too.

An overdue invoice: the gallery owes him about two thousand dollars and it is now sixty-five days old. He said he is not going to chase because he does not want to upset them. I disagreed. Anh said she thinks he should send a polite reminder. Giuseppe said he would "think about it", so there is no commitment. I have noted it as open.

Anh and Giuseppe both offered to share a stand at the trade fair next March. This would save each of them about a third of the cost. They will decide together by Friday 29 October whether to book it. Nobody is yet responsible for finding out whether there is a deposit. I think I will leave that for them.

Naomi. She had a hard fortnight. A staff member, her receptionist Jill, has given notice and finishes on 22 October. Naomi said the booking system is mostly in Jill's head. I asked if there was a handover document. There is not. Naomi will ask Jill to write one this week and will review it with her on Monday 18 October. I also told Naomi, directly, not to hire a replacement in a hurry. She laughed and said she would try.

Naomi asked the group whether she should raise her prices. I said I would wait on that, and she agreed. The topic was parked until the January session. Anh said she raised hers by ten percent in July and "nobody noticed". Giuseppe said he would be nervous. Naomi said, quietly, that she worries about whether the patients can afford it. A background concern, I think, rather than a decision.

Naomi and Anh also had a thought about sharing a part-time bookkeeper. They talked it through for a few minutes and then dropped it, because Naomi's clinic needs someone who knows health billing. Anh said she would not push it. The idea is withdrawn.

Questions raised. Giuseppe asked whether any of us knew a good accountant who works with galleries. Nobody knew one. Naomi said she would ask a friend and tell him. Anh asked, "Why does everything take three times longer than you plan?" That was more of a sigh than a question, and we laughed.

Positive moments. Anh said this was the first group session where she left with a clear plan. Naomi said the group has stopped her from feeling alone. Giuseppe said he valued Naomi's honesty about her prices.

Things I need to do. I will send the group a short summary of agreed actions on Friday 8 October, leaving out Giuseppe's section. I will also write up a one-page worksheet on pricing for the January session. No date for that yet, but before the new year.

One line to remind myself: Anh is the loudest in the room and Giuseppe the quietest. Next time, I should ask him first.

Next session: Wednesday 3 November, same place.
