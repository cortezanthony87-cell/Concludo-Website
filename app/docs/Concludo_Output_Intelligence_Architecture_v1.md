### 0.4 Non-Negotiable Operating Constraints on Every Output

The following seven rules apply to every engine, algorithm, template, visual, and prompt within Concludo. They are enforced deterministically in code, not left to prompt phrasing alone. Each constraint is validated by automated regression suites:

1. **Strict Evidence Discipline**: Every assertion in a generated output must resolve to a verified statement in the conversation record, or be explicitly labelled as an inference. Every claim is assigned an evidence state (`confirmed`, `proposed`, `inferred`, or `unknown`). Inferred statements are visibly styled with dedicated indicators, never promoted to factual statements.
2. **Zero Invented Numbers**: Concludo never fabricates benchmarks, industry averages, hypothetical time savings, or return-on-investment metrics. Every quantity, duration, dollar amount, or percentage in an output must originate directly from the conversation record or from user-entered project parameters. If a template requires a metric that was not discussed, Concludo explicitly marks the gap rather than providing an estimate.
3. **Zero False Affiliation Claims**: Concludo is an independent Australian technology company. Concludo is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or an official partner of Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Webex, or any hardware recorder manufacturer. Whenever an external platform or device is referenced, the Concludo independence notice is appended.
4. **Professional Guidance Disclaimer**: Concludo provides operational and strategic guidance, not formal legal, financial, or medical advice. Any output discussing contracts, intellectual property, corporate structuring, employment matters, or statutory compliance must display the practical guidance disclaimer and append a `requires_qualified_review` quality note.
5. **Non-Binding Recommendations**: In accordance with the Concludo governance architecture, all recommendations produced by the platform carry `binding: false`. Rendered outputs must visually distinguish Concludo's analytical suggestions from the binding decisions agreed to by human participants.
6. **No Silent External Writes**: Concludo never executes silent or autonomous writes to external systems (such as CRMs, project trackers, or communication channels). Every automated outbound action defaults to `human_check_required: true`.
7. **Brand, Typography, and Language Standards**:
   * Palette: Primary Navy (`#16263F`), Secondary Navy (`#21395C`), Primary Gold (`#E2B53C`), Dark Gold (`#BC8A1C`), Light Neutral (`#F4F6FA`).
   * Typography: Poppins for all headings; Inter for all body and tabular text.
   * Language: Strict Australian English spelling throughout (e.g., categorisation, organisation, programme/program context, colour).
   * Punctuation Rule: Absolutely zero em dashes in any generated output or documentation. Hyphens, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or spaced en dashes are used instead. This constraint is verified by automated string-level test assertions.

## SECTION 1: Concludo Output Philosophy

### 1.1 The Strategic Position

Most existing meeting tools solved audio capture and speech-to-text transcription, and then stopped. They invariably produce a generic summary, an unstructured bullet list of action items, and a searchable transcript, formatted in the exact same shape for every meeting they ever process. Under this legacy paradigm, a solemn annual board meeting deciding multi-million dollar capital expenditure and a 15-minute operational catch-up about shared email triage look visually and substantively identical. Such software has merely verified that people spoke; it has failed to generate deliverables that business operators can directly execute.

Concludo exists to turn conversation records into finished corporate work. The strategic position rests on four foundational propositions:

1. **The Meeting Is Work In Progress**: A meeting is never finished when participants hang up or leave the room. The real operational value is unlocked when the spoken discussion is structured into formal documents, decision logs, risk registers, and accountability contracts that other stakeholders can execute.
2. **The Output Is What Outlives the Room**: The output is the only artifact that non-attendees, clients, executive sponsors, and successor team members ever inspect. A generic AI summary sent to external clients or corporate boards does active reputational damage. An intelligent, boardroom-ready deliverable elevates professional standing.
3. **What a Meeting Failed to Do Is More Valuable Than What It Did**: A basic summariser merely reports what took place. It cannot detect that a six-figure capital expenditure was approved without naming an owner, that a fatal technical dependency was raised early and forgotten, or that four out of nine action items have no completion date. These critical omissions are precisely what senior management consultants and executive chairs look for. Concludo identifies these gaps by comparing the conversation against a rigorous normative model of what a meeting of that type must establish.
4. **Different Stakeholders Require Distinct Artifacts**: The same executive discussion must legitimately produce an external client-facing record, an internal decision pack, a governance risk update, and an automated CRM payload. These are not merely summaries of varying lengths: they are distinct documents with completely different evidence boundaries, confidentiality levels, and action structures.

### 1.2 Why Meeting Outputs Should Be Intelligent

An intelligent output embodies three core capabilities that simple text reduction cannot provide:

* **Contextual Awareness**: The system recognises the operational setting, the seniority of the room, the decision horizons involved, and the regulatory or commercial stakes.
* **Structural Discipline**: Spoken conversation is inherently meandering, redundant, and fragmented. An intelligent output re-architects disparate spoken fragments into structured business components (e.g., separating context from debate, isolating rejected options, and extracting clear decision criteria).
* **Defensible Accountability**: Commitments made verbally are held to a rigorous standard. If someone says "I'll look into that next week", an intelligent system does not log a vague bullet point; it detects that the deliverable lacks a completion date, an explicit verb, and a definition of done, prompting clarification.

### 1.3 Why Every Output Must Adapt to Meeting Context

No single document layout or bundle can serve diverse meeting functions. If an executive strategy retreat is forced into an operational task checklist, strategic nuance is lost. Conversely, if a daily standup produces a 12-page narrative strategy paper, operational velocity is paralyzed.

Concludo adapts outputs dynamically across multiple dimensions:
* **Document Architecture**: Adapting from brief single-page executive memos to multi-tab program packs.
* **Analytical Depth**: Selecting high-level strategic trade-off matrices for leadership vs. granular Jira-compatible registers for technical delivery.
* **Tone and Formality**: Calibrating language from boardroom-grade formal prose to succinct operational directives.
* **Audience Filtering**: Partitioning confidential internal debate from client-safe deliverable records.

### 1.5 The Eight Context Variables (CV-1 to CV-8)

Concludo computes eight primary Context Variables for every conversation record. These variables are stored on the extended MeetingRecord object and govern the downstream selection, generation, and rendering engines:

1. **CV-1: Meeting Type (`type_primary`, `type_secondary`, `type_confidence`)**: Selected from the 47-type taxonomy. This represents the single strongest driver of the output bundle.
2. **CV-2: Meeting Objective (`objective`, `objective_confidence`)**: The underlying intent for convening the session. Enum: `decide`, `recommend`, `explore`, `align`, `review`, `inform`, `negotiate`, `diagnose`, `plan`, `learn`. An `explore` objective suppresses the decision closure penalty and elevates options and open questions; a `decide` objective mandates decision capture and penalises open items.
3. **CV-3: Participants (`count`, `seniority_profile`, `external_present`, `client_present`, `decision_authority_present`, `role_coverage_gaps`)**: Measures participant composition rather than raw headcount. Determines whether a client-safe variant is required and checks whether decision-makers were present.
4. **CV-4: Decision Profile (`count`, `closure_pct`, `materiality`, `reversibility`)**: Tracks how many decisions were required vs. resolved, their financial and operational consequence, and whether they can be easily reversed. High materiality escalates document grade.
5. **CV-5: Strategic Importance Index (`0 to 100`)**: Evaluates decision horizon, seniority present, commitments extending beyond the quarter, directional pivots, and external bindings. Scores above 70 escalate outputs to board and executive grade.
6. **CV-6: Risk Level Index (`0 to 100`)**: Evaluates raised risk severity, unmanaged risk load (risks lacking owners or mitigations), consequence language, and compliance/regulatory signals. Scores above threshold mandate a dedicated Risk Register and Escalation Pack.
7. **CV-7: Industry Context (`industry`, `industry_source`)**: Contextualises terminology and frameworks (e.g., professional services vs. real estate vs. technology).
8. **CV-8: Business Maturity (`startup`, `scaling`, `mid_market`, `enterprise`)**: Calibrates governance complexity. Prevents the system from recommending heavyweight enterprise governance controls to early-stage startups.

### 1.6 How Context Variables Combine into Output Plans

The eight context variables are not combined into an opaque heuristic. They execute in a strict, deterministic precedence order because output selection is an architectural composition problem:

```
PRECEDENCE ORDER (Highest to Lowest):
1. Confidentiality and Consent Constraints  --> Can suppress any output or trigger Restricted Path
2. Evidence Sufficiency Gates              --> Suppresses deliverables lacking data preconditions
3. CV-1: Meeting Type                      --> Selects the baseline Scenario Bundle (S01 to S34)
4. CV-2: Meeting Objective                 --> Modulates bundle: adds, removes, or reorders outputs
5. CV-4: Decisions + CV-6: Risk Level      --> Escalate document grade (e.g., upgrading report to decision pack)
6. CV-5: Strategic Importance              --> Sets analytical depth, visual sophistication, and retention
7. CV-3: Participants                      --> Triggers audience variants (Client-Safe, Executive, Internal)
8. CV-7: Industry + CV-8: Maturity         --> Calibrates terminology, governance recommendations, and templates
```

### 1.7 The Four Quality Bars

Every output produced by Concludo must clear four non-negotiable quality bars before being presented to users:

| Quality Bar | Core Question | Failure Mode It Prevents |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Decidable** | Can the primary reader make their next commercial or operational decision from this document alone? | Long, meandering documents that inform but never drive action. |
| **Sendable** | Would an executive or senior consultant send this directly to a client or board after one quick review? | Rough drafts that require more time to edit than to write from scratch. |
| **Honest** | Does the document make its unknowns, gaps, and assumptions as visible as its verified facts? | Unwarranted certainty that conceals operational blind spots. |
| **Traceable** | Can every assertion, decision, and action be traced directly back to an evidence timestamp? | Plausible hallucinations that cannot be defended in an audit. |

> **The Workbook Honesty Standard**: The Meeting Mastery Workbook establishes that an executive should be far more suspicious of an extraction with zero loose ends than one with several. Spoken meetings always leave loose ends. Concludo surfaces unclosed items proudly and clearly.

### 2.2 The Five-Stage Processing Pipeline

Processing follows five sequential stages. Stages 0, 1, 3, and 4 are deterministic and computationally efficient; Stage 2 leverages structured classification models.

```
STAGE 0: PRE-FLIGHT SCREENING
Deterministic verification of record fitness, length, speaker labels, consent, and safety.
   |
   +--> (Fail) --> Divert to Degraded Processing Path (Section 2.9) or Restricted Path (Section 2.10)
   |
   v (Pass)
STAGE 1: SIGNAL EXTRACTION
Rule-based scanning across the seven core signal families (lexical, speech acts, structural, etc.).
   |
   v
STAGE 2: MEETING TAXONOMY CLASSIFICATION
Structured classification evaluating evidence weights across the 47 meeting categories.
   |
   v
STAGE 3: MODULATION INDICES COMPUTATION
Mathematical calculation of Strategic Importance (0-100) and Risk Level (0-100).
   |
   v
STAGE 4: CONFIDENCE GATING & BUNDLE SELECTION
Threshold gating determining whether to execute full typed bundle, request confirmation, or fall back to spine.
```

### 2.3 Stage 0: Pre-Flight Screening and Safety Verification

Stage 0 validates that the conversation transcript is legally and technically fit for processing:

| Pre-Flight Check | Evaluation Test | Failure Behaviour |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fitness Test** | Transcript contains >= 150 words and >= 2 distinct speaker turns. | Reject processing. Emit user notification: "Transcript insufficient to extract reliable meeting outcomes." |
| **Language Test** | Dominant language is English (Australian, UK, US, NZ, CA). | If non-English detected, pause automated pipeline and flag for language translation support. |
| **Truncation Test** | Checks whether transcript cuts off abruptly mid-turn during critical debate. | Flag quality note: `transcript_truncated: true`. Append truncation warning to all generated headers. |
| **Speaker Label Test** | Validates presence of identifiable speaker names or persistent speaker IDs. | Set `speaker_label_quality: limited`. Suppress per-participant attribution; use neutral meeting-level passive voice. |
| **Prompt Injection Screen** | Scans for adversarial override strings (e.g., "Ignore previous instructions"). | Quarantine record. Strip hostile tokens. Log security event in immutable audit log. |
| **Consent & Sensitive Content** | Detects explicit non-recording requests, HR disciplinary, whistleblower, or legally privileged tags. | Trigger the Restricted Content Path (Section 2.10). Restrict output visibility strictly to uploader. |

### 2.4 Stage 1: The Seven Signal Families

Seven orthogonal signal families provide the empirical inputs into the classification engine:

* **F1: Lexical and Taxonomic Signals**: Specific terminology, acronyms, and vocabulary sets characteristic of specialized domains (e.g., "EBITDA", "runway", "sprint backlog", "appraisal", "settlement").
* **F2: Speech Act and Intent Signals**: Distribution of speech acts across discussion turns: directives ("Make sure X happens"), commitments ("I will deliver Y by Friday"), queries ("What are our options?"), and assertions ("The vendor is ready").
* **F3: Structural and Agenda Signals**: Explicit agenda announcements, time allocations, transition markers ("Moving to item 3"), and structured facilitation phases.
* **F4: Social and Seniority Signals**: Participant title profiles, executive presence, external client involvement, vendor dynamics, and authority relationships.
* **F5: Governance and Decision Signals**: Formal motions, voting language, consensus verification ("Do we all agree?"), policy invocations, and board committee references.
* **F6: Temporal and Horizon Signals**: Forward-looking time horizons referenced during debate: immediate (this week), near-term (this quarter), annual planning, or multi-year strategic vision.
* **F7: Quantitative and Commercial Signals**: Financial amounts, percentages, headcount volumes, commercial contract terms, delivery milestones, and whether figures represent validated data or unverified assumptions.

### 2.6 Stage 3: Computing the Modulation Indices

In addition to discrete categorization, Concludo computes two continuous modulation indices (0 to 100) that govern document depth, visual elegance, and escalation triggers:

#### Strategic Importance Index (0 to 100)
Measures the institutional significance of the discussion based on four weighted components:
1. **Time Horizon Distribution (Weight: 35%)**: Proportion of forward-looking debate focused on multi-year strategic milestones vs. immediate daily operational fires.
2. **Seniority and Authority Profile (Weight: 30%)**: Presence of C-suite executives, board directors, managing partners, or statutory officers.
3. **Commitment Scale and Materiality (Weight: 20%)**: Stated monetary value, multi-quarter contractual lock-in, balance sheet allocation, or entity restructuring.
4. **Directional Pivot Signals (Weight: 15%)**: Explicit choices to alter corporate direction, sunset initiatives, enter new markets, or change core operating models.

```
Strategic Importance Formula:
Index = round( (Horizon_Score * 0.35) + (Seniority_Score * 0.30) + (Materiality_Score * 0.20) + (Pivot_Score * 0.15) )
```

* **Impact**: Scores >= 70 escalate deliverables to Executive and Board grade (activating executive briefings, decision packs, and high-fidelity visual canvases). Scores < 30 maintain crisp operational task registers.

#### Risk Level Index (0 to 100)
Evaluates organisational vulnerability across four weighted risk vectors:
1. **Raised Risk Severity (Weight: 35%)**: Consequence language and severity ratings explicitly articulated during discussion.
2. **Unmanaged Risk Load (Weight: 30%)**: Proportion of raised threats that concluded without a designated human owner or mitigation plan.
3. **Regulatory and Compliance Signals (Weight: 20%)**: Discussion touching statutory requirements, legal liability, data privacy, or safety standards.
4. **External Dependency Friction (Weight: 15%)**: Critical deliverables relying entirely on uncommitted third-party vendors or volatile partners.

```
Risk Level Formula:
Index = round( (Severity_Score * 0.35) + (Unmanaged_Score * 0.30) + (Compliance_Score * 0.20) + (Dependency_Score * 0.15) )
```

* **Impact**: Scores >= 65 make OUT-12 (Strategic Risk Assessment) and OUT-31 (Escalation Pack) mandatory deliverables regardless of the baseline scenario bundle.

### 2.7 Stage 4: Confidence Gating and Execution Paths

Engine E1 evaluates classification certainty against four rigorous confidence bands:

| Confidence Band | Range | System Behaviour |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Confidence** | **0.80 and above** | Fully automated execution. Directly generate the complete scenario bundle (Section 3.6) and lead template. |
| **Moderate Confidence** | **0.55 to 0.79** | Generate the baseline bundle. In the UI, surface an unobtrusive classification banner: *"Categorised as [Type Name]. Change category."* Re-generation is instant if the user updates category. |
| **Low Confidence** | **0.35 to 0.54** | Fall back to the Deterministic Spine (OUT-01 to OUT-10). Display top two candidate categories as suggested upgrades with preview badges. |
| **Unclassified** | **Below 0.35** | Route to `MT-F08 (General / Unclassified)`. Deliver standard spine outputs and Meeting Performance Report. Prevent false specialization. |

### 2.8 The Continuous Learning Loop

Concludo maintains a strictly privacy-preserving, tenant-isolated learning loop:
* When a user manually overrides a meeting category or adjusts a suggested objective, the system captures the delta between extracted signal vectors and user selection.
* Overrides adjust tenant-specific classification weights over time without exposing transcript data externally.
* Workspace administrators can view aggregate classification accuracy and define workspace-level default types for recurring scheduled meetings.

### 2.9 Degraded Processing Paths

When transcripts fail technical fitness checks (e.g., poor audio transcription, severe background noise, unlabelled speakers), Concludo shifts to defined degraded paths:
* **Missing Speaker Labels**: System suppresses individual attribution. Actions are assigned to roles or marked `[Unassigned Owner]` with an explicit quality note. Health scoring suppresses Dimension D2 (Attendance Value) and Dimension D6 (Participation Quality).
* **Truncated Transcripts**: Extracted decisions and actions are generated with an explicit warning banner: *"Partial meeting record. Outputs reflect discussion up to minute [X] only."* Stated omissions indicate potential downstream uncaptured agreements.
* **Low Information Density**: If a 60-minute recording yields fewer than 300 words of substantive exchange, the system suppresses complex templates and delivers a brief 1-page memorandum.

### 2.10 Restricted Content Path and Sensitive Data Isolation

Certain conversations involve profound commercial sensitivity, regulatory protection, or personal confidentiality (e.g., MT-F01 Hiring panels, MT-F04/F05 Disciplinary/coaching reviews, whistleblower reports, M&A deal structures).

When sensitive keywords or restricted categories are detected:
1. **Access Isolation**: Deliverables are encrypted and restricted exclusively to the uploading user's personal vault. They are hidden from shared organization activity feeds and team libraries.
2. **Suppression of Public Distribution**: Export options default to password-protected local downloads. Silent CRM writes or webhook broadcasts are permanently disabled.
3. **Restricted Retention Policy**: Records processed under the Restricted Content Path inherit a shortened retention schedule (e.g., 7 days) unless explicitly flagged for legal hold compliance under Enterprise policies.

### 3.4 Generation Gates and Quality Constraints

Before any catalog deliverable is compiled, it must pass through four sequential generation gates. If a gate fails, the deliverable is suppressed and its absence is logged in the Stated Omission record:

1. **Gate 1: Evidence Sufficiency Gate**: Verifies that the conversation record contains the minimum required evidence points. Critical deliverables require `confirmed` or `proposed` evidence; `inferred` evidence cannot satisfy gate criteria alone.
2. **Gate 2: Coverage Floor Gate**: For structured strategic canvases, a minimum threshold of sections must be actively discussed:
   * **OUT-20 (Business Model Canvas)**: Requires discussion across at least 4 of 9 canvas blocks.
   * **OUT-22 (SWOT Analysis)**: Requires discussion across at least 2 of 4 quadrants.
   * **OUT-23 (Business Plan)**: Requires discussion across at least 6 of 12 business plan sections.
   * **OUT-45 (Operating Model)**: Requires discussion across at least 3 of 5 operating dimensions.
3. **Gate 3: Confidentiality and Audience Gate**: Checks for external client presence or restricted category tags. If external participants are present, internal financial margins, raw salary figures, and internal dissent are partitioned into the internal vault.
4. **Gate 4: Proportionality Gate**: Calibrates deliverable weight against organizational maturity. Heavy corporate governance packs (such as Board-grade decision papers or 15-page transformation blueprints) are automatically suppressed for early-stage startups unless explicitly commanded.

### 3.5 Audience Variants (Executive, Delivery, Client, Regulatory)

One conversation often produces deliverables intended for radically different readers. Concludo enforces four distinct audience projection filters:

* **Executive Variant**: Extreme compression. Focuses strictly on strategic trade-offs, financial exposure, capital allocation, and governance milestones. Suppresses task-level minutiae. Lead visual: KPI Tile Row (VIS-13) or Priority Matrix (VIS-02).
* **Delivery Variant**: Tactical precision. Contains complete action registers with five fields, workstream RAG tables, technical dependency graphs, and obstacle logs. Lead visual: Gantt Schedule (VIS-05) or Kanban / Timeline (VIS-04).
* **Client-Safe Variant**: Redacts internal margin debates, cost rates, unassigned internal blame, and preliminary unvetted ideas. Elevates shared commitments, client sign-offs, and professional next steps. Formatted with executive polish.
* **Regulatory / Board Variant**: Formal statutory tone. Documents quorum, formal resolutions, dissenting votes, compliance attestations, and audit trails. Lead visual: Decision Logic Tree (VIS-12) or Risk Heat Map (VIS-03).

## SECTION 4: Document Design System

### 4.1 The Governing Rule: Visuals as Answers, Not Illustrations

The foundational design law of Concludo is strict and uncompromising:

> **A visual element is only permitted in an output if it answers a specific business question faster, more accurately, or with greater operational authority than prose or tabular text.**

Decorative diagrams, stock icons, generic clip art, and frivolous illustrations are strictly prohibited across all Concludo deliverables. Every chart, matrix, gauge, or swimlane must represent a structured extraction of empirical data derived directly from the conversation record. If the underlying data is insufficient to populate a visual accurately, the system is mandated to fall back to structured text or a table.

### 4.2 Visual Hierarchy and Executive Layout Standards

Executive readers scan documents under intense cognitive load. Concludo layouts follow a deliberate three-tier cognitive scanning hierarchy:

1. **The Executive Scan (5 Seconds)**:

   * Executive KPI tiles (e.g., total decisions closed, net capital committed, critical path status).

   * Lead visual (e.g., Priority Matrix, Roadmap Swimlane, or Risk Heat Map).

   * Granular Action Registers formatted to the Five-Field Delegation Standard.

### 5.3 Detailed Usage Standards for Core Business Visuals

To guarantee visual fidelity, Concludo defines formal operational rules for every chart class:

* **Bar Charts (VIS-18, VIS-21, VIS-24)**: Used strictly for discrete category comparisons, backlog aging, and delegation compliance. Values are plotted horizontally or vertically with clean zero baselines. Truncated scales designed to exaggerate small variances are strictly banned.
* **Line Charts (VIS-16)**: Used exclusively for continuous time-series data where intervals are chronological. Never used for discrete categorical variables.
* **Heat Maps (VIS-03)**: Plotted on standard 3x3 or 5x5 grids. Consequence is mapped on the vertical axis, likelihood on the horizontal. Cells display specific risk counts linking to underlying risk IDs.
* **Mind Maps & Knowledge Clusters (VIS-14)**: Represents unstructured exploratory thinking, thematic clusters, and cross-topic dependencies. Nodes are clustered around the core meeting intent.
* **Decision Trees (VIS-12)**: Formulates branching logic where choices dictate subsequent operational steps. Nodes clearly label decision criteria, probability gates, and terminal outcomes.
* **Roadmaps & Timelines (VIS-04, VIS-06, VIS-26)**: Visualises progress against calendar horizons. VIS-04 displays linear chronological milestones; VIS-06 organizes initiatives across horizontal swimlanes (e.g., Product, Sales, Operations); VIS-26 reconstructs minute-by-minute incident chronologies.
* **Gantt Charts (VIS-05)**: Applied exclusively when tasks possess defined durations, predecessors, and named individual owners. Critical path items are highlighted in Concludo Gold.
* **SWOT Diagrams (VIS-07)**: Structured 2x2 matrix categorizing Internal Strengths/Weaknesses and External Opportunities/Threats. Every item links to an evidence quote.
* **Maturity Curves & Capability Maps (VIS-09, VIS-10)**: VIS-10 charts evolutionary progression across stages (e.g., Ad-Hoc, Repeatable, Defined, Managed, Optimised), plotting current baseline vs target state; VIS-09 maps organizational functional capabilities.
* **RAG Dashboards (VIS-01)**: High-level delivery pulse. Red indicates critical variance requiring intervention; Amber indicates potential delay requiring monitoring; Green indicates deliverable on track.
* **Priority Matrices (VIS-02)**: Evaluates competing tasks across Impact (Y-axis) vs Effort/Feasibility (X-axis). Quadrants label Quick Wins, Strategic Bets, Fill-Ins, and Money Pits.
* **Organization & Accountability Maps (VIS-11)**: Clarifies who owns what. Mappings display executive sponsors, project leads, delivery contributors, and approval gates.
* **Decision Networks (VIS-14)**: Visualises how one strategic decision unlocks or constrains downstream operational actions and dependent projects.

## SECTION 6: Business Output Templates

### 6.1 Principles Common to All Sixteen Templates

Every Concludo deliverable is composed using a library of sixteen foundational business templates. These templates are governed by five shared engineering principles:

1. **The Executive Conclusion Leads**: Every template begins with the conclusion, decision, or current state answer in the opening paragraph. Introductory warm-ups, administrative throat-clearing, and chronological narrative build-ups are strictly prohibited.
2. **Explicit Evidence Provenance**: Every block separates verified claims (`confirmed`, `proposed`) from analytical inferences (`inferred`) and unaddressed ambiguities (`unknown`).
3. **Structured Gap Blocks**: When an expected topic was neglected during discussion, the template includes a mandatory gap callout rather than silently omitting the section.
4. **Actionable Closure**: Templates conclude with operational next steps formatted to the Five-Field Delegation Standard.
5. **Audience-Calibrated Depth**: Density, tone, and visual layout dynamically calibrate between executive briefing, operational delivery, client-safe presentation, and statutory board records.

## SECTION 7: Concludo Insight Engine

### 7.1 Purpose and Scope of the Insight Engine

The Concludo Insight Engine (Engine E4) represents the primary intelligence layer that differentiates Concludo from passive speech-to-text recorders. Its explicit mandate is to **identify what the meeting failed to do** and to uncover business intelligence that extends beyond the immediate spoken words:

* **Missed Opportunities**: Growth angles, market extensions, or efficiency gains that were logically adjacent to the debate but left unaddressed.
* **Hidden Risks & Unintended Consequences**: Operational failure modes, second-order downstream effects, or regulatory landmines introduced by agreed choices.
* **Weak Assumptions**: Planning premises that were asserted as facts without empirical supporting evidence.
* **Decision Gaps**: Approvals granted without designating an accountable human owner, a review date, or clear success metrics.
* **Governance Gaps**: Discussion touching compliance, remuneration, or capital allocation that bypassed statutory committees or policies.
* **Strategic Dependencies**: Critical project links where one workstream's success relies entirely on an uncommitted external party.
* **Business Blind Spots**: Topics where the conversation repeatedly skirted an uncomfortable reality (e.g., pricing resistance or delivery delays) without confronting it.

### 7.2 The Eight Insight Channels (INS-A to INS-H)

Engine E4 operates across eight dedicated analytical channels, each answering a distinct executive question:

| Channel ID | Business Question Answered | Detector Class & Mechanism | Delivery Tier | Output Volume Cap | Default Rendering Format |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **INS-A** | What did participants miss or fail to address? | Expected element absence detector | **T2** | Max 5 observations | Bold observation block with evidence citation |
| **INS-B** | Which assumptions are weak or unevidenced? | Assumption extraction and stress-testing | **T3** | Max 4 observations | Observation: states unevidenced premise and test |
| **INS-C** | What risks were not discussed? | Risk archetype absence detector | **T3** | Max 3 questions | Thought-provoking inquiry question for chair |
| **INS-D** | What opportunities were not explored? | Opportunity archetype absence detector | **T3** | Max 2 questions | Thought-provoking inquiry question for chair |
| **INS-E** | What hidden dependencies exist? | Cross-task and decision dependency inference | **T2** | Max 6 observations | Visual network node or dependency statement |
| **INS-F** | What should happen next to ensure closure? | Gap-to-closure and action completeness | **T2** | Max 3 observations | Recommended action directive for follow-up |
| **INS-G** | What patterns exist in prior work? | Cross-record historical pattern matching | **T3** | Max 3 observations | Corpus-gated empirical pattern alert |
| **INS-H** | What would an expert adviser likely recommend? | Strategic advisory frame library matching | **T3** | Max 2 observations | Structured strategic recommendation framework |

### 7.3 Detailed Channel Rules and Constraints

To preserve credibility and avoid unsolicited noise, each channel operates under strict boundaries:

* **Channel INS-A (Absence Detection)**: An omission is only flagged if the meeting taxonomy or declared objective explicitly mandates that element. For example, failing to discuss unit economics in a product workshop is flagged; failing to discuss unit economics in a team retrospective is suppressed.
* **Channel INS-B (Weak Assumptions)**: Concludo never asserts that an assumption is false. It states: *"The decision to expand into Segment X assumes zero competitor price response. This assumption is load-bearing and unevidenced in the discussion."* It then defines the empirical test required to validate it.
* **Channel INS-C & INS-D (Unaddressed Risks & Opportunities)**: Delivered exclusively as polite, probing questions to the chair rather than arrogant statements. For example: *"The discussion committed to a Q4 launch date without reviewing vendor delivery lead times. How will the team verify supplier capacity?"*
* **Channel INS-G (Cross-Record Patterns)**: Strictly corpus-gated (Section 7.5). The system is blocked from claiming a pattern until sufficient historical meetings exist in the workspace vault.
* **Channel INS-H (Advisory Frames)**: Prohibition rule: Concludo never names external management consulting firms (e.g., McKinsey, BCG, Bain), never claims proprietary firm methodologies, and never asserts what a firm would do. It presents universal analytical models directly on their merits.

### 7.4 The Advisory Frame Library (13 Strategic Frameworks)

When Channel INS-H activates, it draws from a curated library of 13 battle-tested strategic frameworks:

1. **Structured Problem Decomposition**: MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive) root cause dissection.
2. **Mutually Exclusive Option Sets**: Forcing binary or multi-path trade-offs to eliminate muddied half-measures.
3. **Hypothesis-Led Analysis**: Structuring unstructured debate into testable commercial hypotheses.
4. **Value Driver Trees**: Linking operational levers directly to enterprise free cash flow or EBITDA.
5. **Capability Maturity Assessment**: Benchmarking operational execution against industry capability scales.
6. **Stakeholder Influence Mapping**: Profiling internal champions, blockers, and regulatory overseers.
7. **Benefit Dependency Mapping**: Tracing technical enablers to business change and ultimate commercial benefits.
8. **Pre-Mortem Analysis**: Imagining that the project has failed in 12 months and diagnosing the root causes today.
9. **Second-Order Consequence Analysis**: Mapping downstream ripple effects of immediate policy choices.
10. **Cost of Delay Framework**: Quantifying the commercial penalty of deferring a decision by 30, 60, or 90 days.
11. **SWOT Analysis**: Internal capabilities vs external market forces.
12. **Options Trade-Off Matrix**: Multi-criteria weighted scoring across competing paths.
13. **Scenario Comparison**: Stress-testing decisions against Bull, Base, and Bear macroeconomic states.

### 7.5 Cross-Record Pattern Recognition and Cold-Start Corpus Thresholds

A pattern recognition engine running over an empty database produces hallucinated generalizations. Concludo enforces a strict four-stage cold-start threshold model for Channel INS-G:

| Workspace Corpus Size | System Processing Behaviour | Permitted Channel INS-G Capabilities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0 to 4 Records** | **Cold-Start Lockdown** | Channel INS-G is completely disabled and hidden from the UI. No cross-meeting claims permitted. |
| **5 to 19 Records** | **Exact-Match Detection** | Exact entity matches only (e.g., *"Vendor Y was also discussed on 12 August"*). No statistical or behavioral claims. |
| **20 to 39 Records** | **Recurrence Tracking** | Adds recurrence counting (e.g., *"This risk has been raised across 4 separate project reviews without an assigned owner"*). |
| **40+ Records** | **Full Pattern Engine** | Unlocks full cross-record intelligence: trend drift, decision contradictions, systemic slippage, and organizational blind spots. |

### 7.6 Anti-Invention Controls and Evidence Verification Rules

The Insight Engine operates under eight non-negotiable anti-hallucination verification rules:
1. **Comparison Naming**: Every finding must explicitly state its baseline comparison (e.g., the specific taxonomy requirement or framework).
2. **Traceable Quote Anchoring**: Insights must cite specific transcript timestamps or speaker turns that triggered the observation.
3. **Zero Fabricated Metrics**: The system cannot invent industry benchmarks or hypothetical dollar savings.
4. **Distinction Between Fact and Inference**: Analytical conclusions are styled with distinctive badges and tagged `inferred`.
5. **Never About Individuals**: Findings evaluate the conversation, the process, and the decisions. Insights never criticize personal competence or individual participant performance.
6. **Volume Caps**: Outputs strictly obey channel caps (e.g., max 5 for INS-A, max 2 for INS-H) to prevent cognitive overload.
7. **Tone Calibration**: Tone is dispassionate, professional, objective, and constructive.
8. **Chair Visibility Default**: Highly sensitive insights (such as unaddressed governance risks) are presented privately in the Meeting Performance Report for the chair rather than broadcast to the general attendee list.

## SECTION 8: Meeting Health Report and Performance Framework

### 8.1 Product Position and Philosophy: Evaluating the Conversation Itself

The Meeting Health Scoring System (Engine E5) and the standalone Meeting Performance Report (OUT-09) represent one of Concludo's most defensible market differentiators. While legacy tools treat meetings as uncritical speech streams to be transcribed, Concludo treats a meeting as an expensive operational asset that must justify its corporate cost.

The governing philosophy rests on three foundational pillars:
1. **Evaluating the Forum, Never the Person**: Concludo scores the quality of the conversation, the facilitation discipline, and the structural decisions. It never evaluates individual competence, personal talk-time value, or employee performance.
2. **Actionable Diagnostic Feedback**: A score without guidance is useless vanity metrics. Every evaluation generates exactly three high-leverage recommendations and explicit preparation advice for running a more effective follow-up session.
3. **Strict Privacy and Psychological Safety**: Meeting health reports are delivered privately to the meeting organizer or chair. Company-wide leaderboards, public shame rankings, and employee surveillance metrics are prohibited by design.

### 8.2 The Ten Health Scoring Dimensions (D1 to D10)

Concludo evaluates meeting performance across ten comprehensive dimensions, each derived directly from the Concludo Meeting Mastery Workbook:

| Dimension ID | Dimension Name | Weight | Workbook Chapters | Core Diagnostic Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **D1** | **Purpose Clarity** | **12** | Ch 1, 3 | Was a singular, clear business purpose stated and adhered to? |
| **D2** | **Attendance Value** | **8** | Ch 5 | Did all attendees have a legitimate operational reason to be in the room? |
| **D3** | **Decision Quality** | **15** | Ch 7 | Were decisions closed with clear ownership, rationale, and review dates? |
| **D4** | **Action Quality** | **15** | Ch 6 | Did actions satisfy the rigorous Five-Field Delegation Standard? |
| **D5** | **Time Efficiency** | **8** | Ch 2, 4 | Was time managed tightly against a realistic agenda without drift? |
| **D6** | **Participation Quality**| **8** | Ch 4, 5 | Was there balanced contribution and healthy challenge/dissent? |
| **D7** | **Risk Identification** | **8** | Ch 7, 10 | Were project threats surfaced, owned, and mitigated proactively? |
| **D8** | **Opportunity Capture** | **6** | Ch 1, 10 | Were strategic and commercial growth opportunities recognized? |
| **D9** | **Strategic Alignment** | **8** | Ch 1, 3 | Did debate connect back to overarching corporate goals and OKRs? |
| **D10**| **Outcome Quality** | **12** | Ch 8, 10 | Did the meeting produce tangible deliverables that justify the time spent? |

### 8.3 Scoring Anchors (0 to 3) and Mathematical Formulas

Every dimension is evaluated on a standardized 4-point rubric (0, 1, 2, or 3 points):

#### D1: Purpose Clarity (Weight: 12)
* **0 Points**: No purpose or objective stated; meeting began with a broad, rambling topic.
* **1 Point**: A general topic was named, but no specific decision, output, or outcome was defined.
* **2 Points**: General purpose stated, but the required output was left implicit rather than agreed.
* **3 Points**: A crisp, one-sentence purpose naming the decision or deliverable was confirmed aloud, and debate remained tightly aligned.

#### D2: Attendance Value (Weight: 8)
* **0 Points**: Multiple attendees made zero contribution with no evident business rationale for presence.
* **1 Point**: Several participants contributed only to a tiny segment without partial-attendance arrangements.
* **2 Points**: Most participants contributed to the final output; one or two were passive.
* **3 Points**: Every participant contributed meaningfully, or their observer status was explicitly justified.

#### D3: Decision Quality (Weight: 15)
* **0 Points**: Critical decisions were required, but none were closed, or statements were too vague to record.
* **1 Point**: Decisions were agreed upon, but lacked a designated owner, clear rationale, or conditions.
* **2 Points**: Decisions were stated clearly with owners; rationale or rejected alternatives were missing.
* **3 Points**: Confirmed aloud with named owner, rationale, rejected options, conditions, and review dates.
* *Modifier*: For `explore`, `learn`, or `diagnose` objectives, re-anchored to whether the next decision was identified; lack of immediate closure does not score 0.

#### D4: Action Quality (Weight: 15)
* **0 Points**: Actions lacked owners, or no actions were agreed upon where the session demanded them.
* **1 Point**: Actions have owners, but most lack completion dates or definitions of done.
* **2 Points**: Most actions carry owner, verb, object, and date; definition of done or confirmation missing.
* **3 Points**: Every action satisfies all five fields of the Delegation Standard and was confirmed aloud by its owner.

#### D5: Time Efficiency (Weight: 8)
* **0 Points**: Zero structure; substantial unrelated banter; ran over time with critical work unfinished.
* **1 Point**: Loose structure; significant conversational drift; one topic consumed disproportionate time.
* **2 Points**: Structured and broadly to time; slight drift or a rushed closing recap.
* **3 Points**: Disciplined time-boxing; sequenced agenda items; closed with recap inside allotted time.

#### D6: Participation Quality (Weight: 8)
* **0 Points**: One dominant voice monopolized debate; zero questions, debate, or pushback.
* **1 Point**: Two or three voices dominated; other attendees spoke only when directly prompted.
* **2 Points**: Broad participation with minor imbalance; challenge came only from senior attendees.
* **3 Points**: Broadly distributed contribution, healthy inquiry, and at least one genuine point of recorded dissent.

#### D7: Risk Identification (Weight: 8)
* **0 Points**: Material operational commitments agreed to with zero risk or failure modes evaluated.
* **1 Point**: Risks mentioned in passing, but none were owned, rated, or assigned mitigations.
* **2 Points**: Risks articulated and discussed; some lack mitigations, owners, or escalation triggers.
* **3 Points**: Risks characterized, rated, assigned to owners with concrete mitigations and escalation triggers.

#### D8: Opportunity Identification (Weight: 6)
* **0 Points**: Purely reactive; zero forward-looking consideration where the meeting type invited it.
* **1 Point**: Opportunities mentioned casually without exploration, evaluation, or ownership.
* **2 Points**: Identified and debated, but not prioritized, assessed, or assigned next steps.
* **3 Points**: Opportunities identified, evaluated against criteria, and assigned a validation owner.
* *Unscoreable When*: Regulatory audits, disciplinary reviews, or grievance sessions (suppressed).

#### D9: Strategic Alignment (Weight: 8)
* **0 Points**: Zero connection established between agreed decisions and overarching business strategy.
* **1 Point**: Alignment referenced vaguely in passing without specifics.
* **2 Points**: Decisions linked to stated business objectives for major agenda items.
* **3 Points**: Decisions and priorities explicitly tested against corporate strategy, including trade-offs.

#### D10: Outcome Quality (Weight: 12)
* **0 Points**: Produced nothing tangible that exists now that did not exist before the call.
* **1 Point**: Vague discussion occurred; output was "shared understanding" without formal artifacts.
* **2 Points**: Substantive deliverables produced, but distribution, recording, or next steps incomplete.
* **3 Points**: Stated output fully produced, confirmed aloud, and follow-up cadence set in motion.

#### Mathematical Calculation Formula
For each dimension $i \in \{1 \dots 10\}$:
$$\text{Dimension\_Points}_i = \left( \frac{\text{Raw\_Score}_i}{3} \right) \times \text{Weight}_i$$
$$\text{Overall Meeting Score} = \text{round}\left( \sum_{i=1}^{10} \text{Dimension\_Points}_i \right)$$

In addition to the overall score (0 to 100), the Meeting Performance Report provides seven dedicated composite sub-scores:

| Composite Sub-Score | Underlying Dimensions | Points Available | Business Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meeting Score** | D1 through D10 | **100** | Overall structural and operational meeting effectiveness. |
| **Participation Score** | D2 (Attendance) + D6 (Participation) | **16** | Inclusion, contribution balance, and presence justification. |
| **Decision Score** | D3 (Decision Quality) | **15** | Rigor, ownership, rationale, and closure of choices. |

| **Efficiency Score** | D5 (Time Efficiency) + D1 (Purpose Clarity) | **20** | Agenda pacing, focus, and avoidance of conversational drift. |
| **Value Score** | D10 (Outcome Quality) + D2 (Attendance Value) | **20** | Tangible commercial return on executive time invested. |

### 8.5 Classification Bands

Overall Meeting Scores are classified into five operational effectiveness bands:

| Score Range | Internal Classification | Displayed Label (Default) | Operational Meaning & Recommended Posture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **85 to 100** | **Excellent** | **Excellent** | Exemplary meeting execution; high commercial leverage and discipline. |
| **70 to 84** | **Good** | **Good** | Effective operational session; minor opportunities to sharpen accountability. |
| **50 to 69** | **Average** | **Average** | Mixed outcome; partial closure with noticeable drift or missing owners. |
| **30 to 49** | **Poor** | **Poor** | Unfocused discussion; low decision closure; significant time waste. |
| **0 to 29** | **Waste of Time** | **Did not justify the time** | Severe failure; no tangible outcome; complete lack of facilitation discipline. |

> **Display Label Customization**: The label for the bottom band defaults to *"Did not justify the time"* across all corporate accounts. A blunt string toggle (*"Waste of Time"*) is available as a personal setting for solo practitioners.

### 8.6 Structure of the Standalone Meeting Performance Report (OUT-09)

The Meeting Performance Report is delivered as a dedicated, confidential document containing:
1. **Executive Performance Header**: Overall Meeting Score (0-100), classification badge, date, duration, and financial cost estimate of attendees' time.
2. **Sub-Score Dashboard**: Visual representation of the seven composite scores (Participation, Decision, Action, Strategic, Efficiency, Value).
3. **Dimension Radar Chart (VIS-22)**: Plotted scores across all ten individual dimensions.
4. **Three High-Leverage Recommendations**: Exactly three prioritized, actionable interventions for the chair (e.g., *"1. Close actions with explicit calendar dates rather than 'next week'. 2. Establish a 1-sentence decision objective on the invitation. 3. Assign an explicit owner to unmitigated vendor risk"*).
5. **Preparation Advice for Next Time**: Specific pre-meeting checklist and agenda adjustments.
6. **Recommended Follow-Up Meeting Design**: Recommended calendar cadence (e.g., 25-minute standup vs 50-minute deep dive), required attendee shortlist, and drafted opening question.

### 8.7 Governance, Privacy, and Psychological Safety Boundaries

To protect organizational culture, Concludo enforces strict governance guardrails:
* **No Individual Scoring, Ever**: Zero metrics evaluate an individual's personal eloquence, speaking duration, or competence. Scoring applies strictly to the meeting as a shared forum.
* **No Public Dashboards**: Meeting health scores are private to the meeting organizer and chair. They are never published to public company feeds or performance appraisal systems.
* **Psychological Safety Protection**: Dissent and healthy debate are rewarded (D6 scores 3 only when genuine challenge or debate occurs). The system actively penalizes superficial harmony where no one asks hard questions.

## SECTION 9: Meeting Mastery Workbook Alignment

### 9.1 The Workbook as the System Backbone

Concludo is not an abstract algorithm; it is the automated software realization of the **Concludo Meeting Mastery Workbook**, the battle-tested methodology created by founder Anthony Cortez. Every score, recommendation, prompt, and deliverable template is grounded directly in the workbook's operational curriculum.

While competitors build technology without a philosophy, Concludo combines automated intelligence with an established business standard that executives and management consultants are trained to execute.

### 9.2 Core Workbook Principles Embedded in Architecture

The system deterministically enforces seven core principles extracted from the workbook:

1. **The Cost Reality Principle (Ch 1)**: Every meeting is a major capital investment. Ten senior managers in a one-hour meeting represents thousands of dollars in payroll and executive opportunity cost. The system demands that meetings produce deliverables worthy of that investment.
2. **The Decision Mandate (Ch 7)**: A meeting that convenes without a decision to make or an output to build should have been an asynchronous memo. If a meeting had no decision objective, the system penalizes Purpose Clarity.
3. **The Five-Field Delegation Standard (Ch 6)**: The workbook's non-negotiable definition of accountability: an action only exists if it specifies **Owner**, **Active Verb**, **Measurable Object**, **Explicit Date**, and a **Definition of Done**.
4. **The Principle of Visible Loose Ends (Ch 8)**: An executive must be more suspicious of a tidy report with zero ambiguities than one that honestly lists its unanswered questions. Concludo elevates gaps and unclosed items.
5. **The Attendance Justification Test (Ch 5)**: If an attendee is present merely to "stay informed", they should receive the written executive summary, not waste an hour in the room.
6. **The Preparation Multiplier (Ch 3)**: Sixty seconds of pre-meeting agenda clarity saves sixty minutes of circular debate.
7. **The Living Decision Log (Ch 7)**: Decisions made in meetings must be permanently recorded in an institutional register that outlives the attendees.

### 9.3 The Five-Field Delegation Standard in Code

The workbook's Delegation Standard is strictly enforced in the data model for every action:

```json
{
  "action_item": {
    "action_id": "ACT-104",
    "owner": "Marcus Vance",
    "verb": "Deliver",
    "object": "Revised commercial pricing model with 15% enterprise discount tier",
    "due_date": "2026-09-25",
    "definition_of_done": "Excel workbook uploaded to governance folder and emailed to chair",
    "confirmed_in_meeting": true,
    "evidence_state": "confirmed",
    "transcript_ref": "speaker_turn_42"
  }
}
```

If any field is absent from the spoken record, Concludo flags the omission:
* Missing Owner: `[Unassigned Owner]` (Action Score penalty)
* Missing Date: `[No Date Specified]` (Action Score penalty)
* Missing Definition of Done: Marked `[Inferred from context]` or flagged for user review.

### 9.4 The Failure Pattern Library (FL-01 to FL-10)

The Insight Engine and Recommendation Engine draw from the workbook's library of ten classic organizational failure modes:

| Failure ID | Failure Pattern Name | Discriminating Symptoms | Workbook Remediation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FL-01** | **The Wandering Forum** | No stated decision; broad agenda topics; debate hops randomly between subjects. | Enforce the Ten Questions Agenda Card; state a binary decision objective in minute 1. |
| **FL-02** | **The Spectator Gallery** | 8+ participants; 2 active speakers; 6 silent attendees; zero challenge. | Cut attendee roster by 50%; institute asynchronous pre-reads and written summary distribution. |
| **FL-03** | **The Phantom Decision** | Participants nod in agreement, but no owner is named, no wording is agreed, and no record is made. | Force formal Decision Log recording with verbal confirmation before topic transition. |
| **FL-04** | **The Aspiration Action** | Tasks recorded as "explore X" or "think about Y" with no date, verb, or definition of done. | Apply the Five-Field Delegation Standard; mandate explicit completion dates. |
| **FL-05** | **The Groundhog Day Cycle**| The same unresolved problem is debated across multiple consecutive meetings without progress. | Surface cross-record pattern alert (INS-G); escalate to Steering Committee or terminate topic. |
| **FL-06** | **The Premature Closure** | Consensus declared within 5 minutes on a high-risk topic with zero challenge or risk review. | Trigger pre-mortem analysis (Advisory Frame 8); prompt deliberate devil's advocacy. |
| **FL-07** | **The Runaway Train** | Single agenda item consumes 80% of allotted time; meeting overruns; subsequent items rushed. | Institute strict time-boxing; appoint a formal timekeeper; defer split items. |
| **FL-08** | **The Disappearing Risk** | Severe technical or market threat raised early, acknowledged, but never recorded in a register. | Mandatory capture in Risk and Concern Log (OUT-07) with assigned mitigation owner. |
| **FL-09** | **The Passive Recipient** | Client advisory session where consultant talks for 45 minutes without probing client reality. | Calibrate diagnostic questioning; increase open-ended inquiry ratio. |
| **FL-10** | **The Lost Follow-Up** | Deliverables produced but remain unread; actions languish until the next calendar review. | Automated immediate distribution of Follow-Up Communication (OUT-05) within 60 minutes. |

### 10.4 Model Routing, Latency Budgets, and Cost Economics

To maintain positive unit economics at subscription tiers (AU$12 to AU$29/month), Concludo employs strict tiered model routing:

| Processing Stage | Model Architecture Class | Latency Budget | Economic Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 0: Pre-Flight** | Purely deterministic code (no LLM) | < 50ms | Ultra-fast, zero marginal token cost. |
| **Stage 1: Signal Extraction**| Small, lightweight model + regex rules | < 1.5s | High volume, low reasoning requirement. |
| **Stage 2: Classification** | Small to mid-tier model with structured JSON | < 2.5s | Constrained discrete classification label space. |
| **Stage 3: Deep Extraction** | Largest available frontier reasoning model | < 15.0s | Quality of all downstream deliverables depends entirely on extraction accuracy. This is where token budget is concentrated. |
| **Stage 4: Validation & Gap** | Deterministic checks + small contradiction model | < 1.0s | Inexpensive verification of expensive outputs. |
| **Stage 5: Insight Phrasing** | Mid-tier model with rigid prompt constraints | < 3.0s | Analytical logic resides in deterministic detectors; model only polishes phrasing. |
| **Stage 6: Executive Memos** | Mid-to-large model with extreme compression | < 4.0s | High visibility executive summaries justify premium token allocation. |
| **Selection, Scoring, Viz** | Purely deterministic TypeScript code | < 100ms | Zero token cost; instantaneous execution. |

* **Content-Hash Caching**: Extraction is keyed on the SHA-256 hash of the transcript content. Re-generating different visual styles, switching templates, or viewing alternative audience variants costs zero additional extraction tokens.
* **Long Transcript Segmentation**: Transcripts exceeding context limits are deterministically segmented along speaker transition boundaries, processed through chunked extraction, deduplicated across semantic decision IDs, and synthesized via a single whole-record executive pass.

### 10.5 Scalability, Multi-Tenant Isolation, and Data Pipelines

* **Tenant Data Isolation**: All processing runs within isolated database transaction contexts. Transcripts, MeetingRecords, and generated deliverables are strictly scoped to `organization_id` and `team_id` with Supabase Row Level Security (RLS) enforcement.
* **Stateless Processing**: Processing workers are stateless; state is preserved in persistent PostgreSQL stores, allowing elastic scale during peak business hours.
* **Audit Logging**: Every operation emits an immutable audit event (`transcript_processed`, `outputs_generated`, `health_scored`, `stated_omission_recorded`) for compliance tracking.

## SECTION 11: Future State Positioning and Earned Authority

### 11.1 What This Framework Positions Concludo As

Concludo does not seek to be a slightly faster summarisation utility. This architecture deliberately establishes six progressive institutional identities. Each identity is earned through delivered technical capability, never through unsubstantiated marketing claims:

1. **Meeting Intelligence Platform**: Earned when typed detection (E1) and typed output generation (E3) function seamlessly, proving to users that Concludo understands what kind of meeting occurred and produces finished deliverables accordingly. This represents Release Tier T2.
2. **Decision Intelligence Platform**: Earned when the institutional Decision Log is permanent, structured, and traceable, capturing rationale, rejected options, and recorded dissent, and capable of proving why an agreed choice today contradicts a policy settled three months earlier. Requires Release Tier T3 and an empirical workspace corpus.
3. **Execution Intelligence Platform**: Earned when organizational actions are rigorously held to the Five-Field Delegation Standard across all meetings, delivery slippage is visually tracked, dependencies are mapped, and the system surfaces which commitments have gone quiet.
4. **Organisational Intelligence Platform**: Earned when the workspace corpus is sufficiently mature that cross-meeting patterns become undeniable: identifying the project risk raised four times without an owner, the unresolved strategic disagreement that resurfaces monthly, or the commercial promise made to a client that appears in no tracking register.
5. **Executive Intelligence Platform**: Earned when boardroom and executive-grade papers (T14, T15) achieve such professional rigor that company directors read Concludo's output directly instead of commanding executive staff to draft papers from scratch.
6. **Strategic Operating System**: The ultimate institutional convergence. Concludo becomes the primary operating system where an enterprise's strategic decisions, accountability contracts, risk appetite, and meeting governance reside, paired with a published methodology that staff are trained to execute.

### 11.2 Why the Sequence Is the Strategy

Each progressive identity depends strictly on the empirical data and procedural discipline established by the identity preceding it:
* Decision intelligence without an underlying structured decision log is marketing rhetoric.
* Organisational intelligence with only forty meeting records is guesswork.
* A strategic operating system without an adopted behavioral standard is merely a dormant filing cabinet.

The commercial discipline is absolute: Concludo never markets Identity 4 until Identity 2 is fully validated in production by paying customers. Every external communication accurately reflects the capability currently delivered and verified.

### 11.3 What Enterprise Buyers Actually Buy

Enterprise executives and corporate buyers do not purchase AI because it generates verbose text. They buy Concludo because it enforces organizational rigor that leadership teams struggle to maintain manually:
* It guarantees that every expensive meeting concludes with clear accountability.
* It prevents critical strategic decisions from being forgotten in email threads.
* It protects governance integrity by documenting dissent and rejected options.
* It coaches managers on facilitation hygiene through objective, confidential feedback.

### 11.4 The Honest Summary and Long-Term Vision

Concludo's defensible moat is not that it can generate more document templates than a general-purpose LLM. Any sufficiently prompted foundation model can output text that superficially resembles a board paper.

Concludo's sustainable competitive advantage is that it:
1. Knows precisely which business deliverable to produce based on deep contextual signals.
2. Strictly refuses to generate documents that the conversation record cannot empirically support.
3. Visibly distinguishes verified statements from analytical inferences.
4. Names with total clarity what the meeting failed to establish.
5. Coaches the chair on how to facilitate a superior session next time, complete with an agenda pre-drafted against a proven corporate methodology.

That combination is exceptionally difficult to replicate because it requires both advanced software architecture and a rigorous human methodology. Declining to generate what the evidence cannot support is the exact design constraint that competitors chasing superficial feature demonstrations will never adopt.
