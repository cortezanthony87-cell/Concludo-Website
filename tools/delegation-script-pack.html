Delegation scripts

An action without a single named owner, a defined outcome, a date and a confirmation method is not an action. It is a hope with good grammar.

The standard · five fields

This is where most meeting value is lost. A meeting can be well designed, well attended and well recorded and still produce nothing, because the actions were written in a form nobody can be held to. Every action carries five fields.

1
Owner. One name. Never a team, never "we".
2
Verb. What the owner will actually do: draft, send, approve, book, call, confirm, decide.
3
Object, with a definition of done. What exists at the end, and what makes it acceptable.
4
Deadline. A date, and a time where the date is not enough.
5
Confirmation method. How and where completion becomes visible, and to whom.
Vague versus accountable

A hope with good grammar

"We'll get the proposal over to them soon."

"Someone should follow up with the client."

"Let's tidy up the onboarding process."

Missing, in order: owner and date. Owner. Definition of done.

An action

"Priya will send the draft proposal to Dan by 5pm Thursday, and post it in the deal channel when it goes."

"Marco will call the client on Tuesday and put the outcome in the account file."

"Priya will rewrite the onboarding checklist so a new starter can run day one unassisted, by the 14th, and share it for comment."

Owner, verb, object with done, date, confirmation.
Script 1 · Assigning in the room
Say it so the owner confirms itOut loud, before anyone moves on
You sayPriya, can you take this one? What I am asking for is the draft proposal sent to Dan, good enough for him to send to the client without rewriting it. Does Thursday 5pm work, and can you drop it in the deal channel when it goes?
Then waitLet Priya answer before you move on. If she says Friday, the action is Friday.
Why it works. The five fields are all in one sentence, and it ends with a question rather than an instruction, so the owner has to accept it out loud. An action nobody said yes to is not owned.
Script 2 · Confirming in writing
Same day, in the follow upWithin twenty four hours
You writeActions from today:

1. Priya to send the draft proposal to Dan by Thu 14th, 5pm. Confirmation: posted in the deal channel.
2. Marco to call the client Tue 12th and record the outcome in the account file.

If I have any of these wrong, tell me by end of tomorrow and I will correct the record.
Why it works. The correction deadline matters as much as the actions. It turns silence into agreement at a defined point, so nobody can say later that they never accepted it.
Script 3 · Chasing before the deadline
Support, not surveillanceA day or two before it is due
You say or writeHi Priya, the proposal draft is due Thursday. Are you on track, or is there anything in the way I can clear? Happy to move the date if something more urgent landed, I would just rather know now than Thursday afternoon.
Why it works. It arrives before the miss, not after it, and it offers to move the date. A renegotiated deadline agreed on Tuesday is worth far more than a silent miss discovered on Friday. Chasing after the fact reads as surveillance; chasing before it reads as help.
Script 4 · Escalating a repeat miss
When a deadline has been missed twiceBack to the decision maker, not chased again
To the owner, firstPriya, this is the second time the proposal has moved. I do not think chasing it a third time is the answer. Either something is blocking it that we have not named, or it is not the right priority for you at the moment. Which is it? I would rather reassign it than keep asking.
Then, if needed, to the decision makerDan, the proposal action has now missed two agreed dates. I have raised it with Priya twice. I am handing it back to you to decide: reassign it, resource it, or drop it. My recommendation is [X].
Why it works. There is a defined point where an action stops being chased and goes back to whoever decided it should exist. Without that point, a stuck action can absorb months of nagging and still not get done, and the relationship pays for it.
Script 5 · When you cannot commit to it
The other side of delegationSay it in the meeting, not two weeks later
You sayI can take that, but not by Thursday, because the audit is due the same day. I can do it by Tuesday the 19th, or I can do a rough version by Thursday that will need someone else to finish. Which is more useful?
Why it works. It accepts ownership while renegotiating the field that is actually wrong, and it offers a choice rather than a refusal. Saying this in the room costs you thirty seconds of discomfort. Saying nothing costs the team two weeks and the trust that comes with it.

Five ways this goes wrong

  • Group ownership. If it belongs to everyone, it belongs to nobody.
  • The action that is really a decision in disguise, handed to someone with no authority to make it.
  • Deadlines set to the next meeting rather than to when the work is actually needed.
  • Confirmation methods that require the chaser to go looking.
  • Recording actions somewhere the team does not already look.
