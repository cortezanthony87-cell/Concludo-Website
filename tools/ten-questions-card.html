The Ten Questions

A meeting that cannot answer ten questions is not a meeting, it is an appointment. These are not a facilitation technique. They are the minimum specification.

Before the meeting
  1. 1
    Purpose. What decision or outcome must this meeting produce, in one sentence.
  2. 2
    Alternative. Could this be a written brief, a shared document or a short call instead.
  3. 3
    People. Who is essential to the decision, and who only needs the outcome afterwards.
  4. 4
    Preparation. What must attendees read, prepare or bring, and by when.
  5. 5
    Agenda and time box. What questions will we work through, in what order, and how long does each get.
During the meeting
  1. 6
    Roles. Who chairs, who records, who keeps time, and who decides.
  2. 7
    Decision. What are we deciding now, and what are we deliberately parking.
  3. 8
    Actions. Who owns each action, by what date, and how will completion be confirmed.
After the meeting
  1. 9
    Follow up. Was the record of decisions, owners and dates sent within twenty four hours, to the people who need it.
  2. 10
    Record and review. Where is the decision recorded, and did this meeting earn its place in the calendar again.

Five ways this goes wrong

  • Answering purpose with a topic. A topic invites discussion; a purpose names the decision.
  • Treating the questions as a checklist to complete rather than a specification to satisfy.
  • Letting question 3 expand instead of contract.
  • Skipping question 7, so nothing is formally parked and everything returns next week.
  • Answering question 10 with attendance.
